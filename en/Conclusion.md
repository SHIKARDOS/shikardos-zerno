# CONCLUSION

The study was devoted to the development of an integrative methodology for creating new solutions — the Grain Principle, based on the triad 集 (Collection), 合 (Synthesis), 生 (Birth).

The following results were obtained.

First, the scientific problem was grounded: the separate components of the innovation process — idea generation, functional description, the analysis of contradictions, knowledge recombination, verification — exist separately, and there is no procedure binding them into a single reproducible cycle.

Second, it was shown that the triad 集 — 合 — 生 has foundations in the philosophical (potency and act, dialectical sublation, emergence), systemic, evolutionary, and cognitive traditions, and the philosophical meaning of each stage was fixed.

Third, the theoretical model of the method was formulated — a closed cycle of seven steps, from task definition to verification and feedback — and operational definitions were given of the key notions, above all the notion of the "grain" as a function separated from its carrier.

Fourth, the stages of the method were formalized: collection (with three directions of search and the blind-bee rule), the Grain Map, synthesis (with the analysis of compatibility and the criterion of wholeness), birth (with the operationalization of new quality), and verification (with a system of criteria and a protocol of comparison).

Fifth, the application of the method was considered: its illustration on the SHIKARDOS tasks and the role of artificial intelligence as a tool, but not a source of truth.

The working hypothesis — that an organized cycle of collection, description, synthesis, and verification raises the transparency, reproducibility, and productivity of innovation search for certain classes of tasks — received illustrative confirmation consistent with it, but was not proved in the strict experimental sense. Full verification on independent tasks with a controlled protocol constitutes the subject of further research.

The boundaries of applicability of the method are outlined honestly: it is not a universal law, does not remove the role of the person, and does not guarantee the emergence of a new quality for any combination of elements.

The Grain Principle, applied to itself, completes the work not with an outcome but with a new question — about the exact conditions under which the method gives an advantage. This question, like the whole path traveled, returns to the beginning of the cycle: collection, synthesis, birth, verification.
