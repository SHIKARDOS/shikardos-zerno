# CHAPTER 14. REFLEXIVE APPLICATION, LIMITATIONS, ETHICS, AND PROSPECTS FOR FURTHER RESEARCH

## 14.1. Reflexive application: the method applied to itself

The present dissertation not only describes the Grain Principle — it was created with its help. This circumstance constitutes its methodological peculiarity and requires separate reflection.

Collection showed itself in the study of preceding approaches: the economic theory of innovation, engineering methodologies, cognitive models of creativity, research on knowledge recombination. The Grain Map showed itself in isolating from these approaches separate functions: the generation of variants, functional description, the analysis of contradictions, verification. Synthesis showed itself in joining these functions into a single cycle. Birth showed itself in the triad 集 — 合 — 生 itself and its definitions. Verification showed itself in the criteria formulated before the writing of the text.

However, the reflexive application, as was warned in the first chapter, does not prove the effectiveness of the method. It only shows that the method is internally applicable to the task of its own construction. Between "the method allowed the text to be written" and "the method surpasses alternatives" lies the same gulf as in any self-description.

Therefore the reflexive application is considered here not as evidence of strength but as a test of internal consistency: a method that could not be applied to its own task would be suspect. Applicability to itself is a necessary but not sufficient condition of soundness.

## 14.2. The limitations of the study

The study has substantial limitations that must be named directly.

The first is the absence of a full-scale experimental verification. The comparative evaluation presented in Chapter 13 is illustrative in character: it rests on real experience, but not on a controlled experiment with independent evaluators and a pre-fixed protocol. The strict confirmation of the working hypothesis remains a task for the future.

The second is dependence on a concrete proving ground. The application of the methodology is shown on one project; how far the conclusions transfer to other fields is unknown and is not asserted.

The third is the role of the researcher. The method formalizes the process but does not remove the figure of the person: the statement of the task, the judgment of compatibility, the recognition of new quality remain acts of responsibility. The methodology lowers arbitrariness, but does not abolish it fully.

The fourth is the incompleteness of the classifications. The classification of functions and the system of criteria are working, not exhaustive; they may be refined or revised.

These limitations do not diminish the result but determine its status: the work proposes a grounded and consistent methodology, not a theory proved in the strict sense.

## 14.3. Ethics and responsibility

A methodology describing the creation of the new cannot evade the ethical dimension. The joining of elements from different fields is also the transfer of responsibility: a borrowed solution carries with it not only a function but also the consequences of its application.

The present work fixes the following ethical positions. The first is honesty about origin: every borrowing indicates its source; novelty is not ascribed to what has been borrowed. The second is honesty about boundaries: the method is not declared a universal law; its applicability is limited to verified conditions. The third is the separation of author and tool: the role of artificial intelligence is stated explicitly, and the responsibility for the result is borne by the person, not the machine.

A special position concerns the reflexive application. Self-description is inclined toward self-aggrandizement; a method describing itself risks passing consistency off as truth. Against this are directed the procedures fixed in the work: the journal of the origin of ideas, the recording of failures, the separation of assumption and verification, external evaluation.

The ethics of the Grain Principle in the end reduce to one requirement: the method must remain a means of honest creativity, and not a cover for passing off the borrowed as one's own or the plausible as the proven.

## 14.4. Prospects for further research

The work opens several directions of continuation.

The first is experimental verification. The methodology must be tested on independent tasks with a formal protocol, control, and independent evaluation. This is the decisive step in the transition from grounding to confirmation.

The second is the widening of proving grounds. The application of the method beyond one project — in engineering, organization, science — will allow outlining the boundaries of applicability more precisely.

The third is software implementation. The development of tools serving collection, the map, and synthesis will allow applying the method to tasks of a larger scale, while retaining for the person the functions of judgment and responsibility.

The fourth is the refinement of notions. The operationalization of "new quality" and of the "grain" may be deepened on the material of concrete studies of emergence and functional analysis.

Finally, there remains open the question posed in the first chapter and not closed to the end anywhere in the work: under which exact conditions the method gives an advantage, and under which it is neutral or loses. The answer to it is not a completion but the next turn of the same cycle: collection, synthesis, birth, verification. The Grain Principle, applied to itself, leads not to an outcome but to a new question.
