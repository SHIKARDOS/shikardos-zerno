# CHAPTER 10. FORMALIZATION OF THE 生 (BIRTH) STAGE

## 10.1. The purpose of the birth stage

Birth is the transition from the functional structure built at the stage of synthesis to a concrete solution possessing testable properties. If synthesis gave the design — a description of grains and links — then birth gives its embodiment.

The name of the stage deliberately points to the main thing: here something must arise that was not in the source elements. Yet birth in the Grain Principle is understood not as a mystical act but as an observable transition — from structure to form, from potency to act. This transition can and must be recorded.

The task of the chapter is to formalize birth so that it does not remain a metaphor: to define what exactly occurs at this stage, what counts as a new quality, and how to make sure that it has arisen.

## 10.2. The embodiment of the structure in a form

The functional structure built by synthesis is not yet a solution. It describes which functions must be performed and how they are linked, but does not prescribe in which material, sign, or organization they will be embodied.

Embodiment is the choice of a concrete form for each function of the structure. The grain of "energy accumulation" may be embodied in a spring, in an accumulator, in a vessel with liquid, or in an information buffer — depending on the constraints of the task. Birth chooses a form satisfying the conditions: the form must realize the function, fit within the constraints, and be compatible with the forms of the remaining grains.

The choice of form is the point where the method opens up to engineering and technological concreteness. The Grain Principle does not prescribe which material to choose — it requires that the choice be functionally justified and recorded. A form is chosen for a function, and not the other way around; if a form begins to dictate the function, this is a sign of an error at the previous stage.

## 10.3. The operationalization of "new quality"

The notion of "new quality" requires a precise definition, otherwise birth cannot be told apart from the simple repetition of the known. In the present work new quality is operationalized as follows:

**A solution possesses a new quality if it performs a function or demonstrates a property that is absent from each of the source grains separately and does not follow from their simple enumeration.**

From this definition flow three testable signs of a new quality:

1. **Irreducibility.** The property of the whole is not observed in any element separately. Water is neither hydrogen nor oxygen; their joining gives a property absent from the parts.

2. **Novelty relative to the sources.** The function or property was not recorded in any of the collected specimens separately. If the solution merely repeats an already-found grain, there is no new quality.

3. **Observability.** The property must be recorded measurably or at least observably, and not declared by a metaphor. The statement "the system has become more harmonious" is not an observation of a new quality until it is indicated which function or measurable characteristic has changed.

The third condition is the strictest and the most important. It draws the boundary between scientific judgment and poetic description. The method recognizes birth as accomplished only when the new quality can be pointed out and verified.

## 10.4. Birth and realization: the difference

It is necessary to distinguish the birth of a new quality from the technical realization of a design.

Realization is the embodiment of an already-known structure: if the design is fully determined and is merely carried into material, this is realization, not birth. Birth occurs when, in the course of embodiment or as a result of joining, a function or property is discovered that was not foreseen by the original design.

This distinction is not evaluative: realization is no worse than birth. But the method is obliged to distinguish them honestly, because what is verified depends on it. If birth is claimed and only realization occurred, the claim is unconfirmed, and this must be reflected in verification.

Practically birth is recorded through a journal: the researcher writes what was foreseen by the design and what was discovered beyond it. It is precisely the divergence between design and result — the appearance of an unforeseen function — that serves as the primary evidence of birth.

## 10.5. The birth procedure: steps

Birth is performed in the following sequence.

**Step 1. Embody the structure.** For each function of the structure a concrete form is chosen and realized, satisfying the constraints.

**Step 2. Record the design.** Before embodiment it is written which functions and properties are expected from the solution. This record serves as the benchmark for comparison.

**Step 3. Join the forms.** The embodied grains are joined in accordance with the links set at the stage of synthesis.

**Step 4. Observe the result.** The obtained solution is tested; all observed functions and properties are recorded — both the expected and the unforeseen.

**Step 5. Compare with the design.** The observed is compared with the recorded design. The appearance of a function absent from the design and from the source grains is a sign of the birth of a new quality.

**Step 6. Document the birth.** The result, the divergence from the design, and the recorded new quality are described so that they can be verified independently.

## 10.6. The transition to verification

Birth completes the creative part of the cycle, but not the cycle itself. The recorded new quality is so far only a claim — it requires confirmation.

Therefore birth passes directly into verification. The new quality recorded at the birth stage becomes the subject of evaluation by the criteria of novelty, usefulness, wholeness, realizability, reproducibility, productivity, and resource efficiency. If verification does not confirm what was claimed, the solution returns to the corresponding stage — most often to synthesis or to the choice of form — and the cycle repeats.

Thus birth is not the completion of the work but only its turning point: from construction to testing. The organization of verification is the subject of the next chapter.
